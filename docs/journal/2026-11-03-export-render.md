# Rendering and encoding an export (M5 slice 2)

- **One function now:** `export` takes a format and a size, and replaces M1's `export_jpeg`. No screens yet; the Export dialog comes in slice 4.
- **`mozjpeg` didn't earn its way in.** The plan asked for a comparison with `jpeg-encoder`. At the same quality setting `mozjpeg`'s files were 14 to 22% smaller, but they scored lower. At a matching score it was 12% larger on one iPhone photo and 9% smaller on the other, and it took about 8 to 10 times as long, in a build without its SIMD because NASM isn't on the PC. Claude Code kept `jpeg-encoder`, as the plan said to without a clear win. The numbers and their limits are in `docs/benchmarks/m5-export.md`.
- **Scaling happens in linear light (Claude Code's choice).** The thumbnails scale the encoded values, which is fine at their size, but it darkens fine detail: black and white stripes come out as 128 rather than 188. The plan only said to resize once, after the render. A sharp edge can ring a little in dark areas with this method, which is its known cost; the first look at real exports should watch for it.
- **Two more settings measured:** progressive JPEGs came out larger, so exports stay baseline. `jpeg-encoder` reduces color detail below quality 90, so the file size can step down between 90 and 89. That's worth remembering for the dialog in slice 4.
- **48 megapixels fits:** at most about 1.1 GB on Windows and 1.3 GB on the Mac, measured differently, and 1.8 and 1.3 seconds for a full-size JPEG. A full-size PNG took 10 and 7 seconds, which is why batches need the progress of slice 4.
- **Windows' own decoder reads the results:** a Display P3 test photo's exports, in both formats, have the right size and one profile each, and its corners clip to sRGB's primaries. That checks the files, not how they look in Photos, which waits until there's a real photo to export.
- **On the Mac,** the Mac session ran the tests, which passed, and the 48-megapixel exports. `sips` reports the same test photo's exports as sRGB in both formats. It used a separate worktree, since the main checkout there was on another branch. The test photos' checksums were unchanged afterwards.
- **A test's wrong guess:** the stripes first failed at 180 where 188 was expected. That was only at the image's edge, where the filter has one side to work with, so the test now looks away from the edges.
- **The reference images** now also go through sRGB and a PNG, read back without Seura's decoders, within one level. A JPEG isn't compared this way, since its losses on such small sharp images say nothing about color, so JPEG is checked on a smooth image, within 5 levels.
