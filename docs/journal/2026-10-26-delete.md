# Delete to Trash (M3 slice 9)

- **Cloud-only photos are left out of Delete for now (Nevada, 2026-10-26).** The measurements (`2026-10-26-trash-measurements.md`) showed they can't reach the Recycle Bin or Trash on either platform. Claude Code set out two choices: leave them out with a notice, keeping rule 5 exactly, or delete them anyway with a warning, as Finder does, reading rule 5 as "never permanent". Nevada agreed to build Delete for photos on disk now, leave cloud-only ones out, and take the choice to a design session. The confirmation and the notice say why they're left and what to do: delete them from the cloud service, or download them first.
- **One system operation for a whole selection.** On Windows each Recycle Bin call took 150 to 200 ms in the measurements, so a hundred photos one by one would take half a minute. Photos and their edit files go in one call. Afterwards, whatever is no longer in its folder counts as deleted, and whatever is still there is reported, whatever the system said, so a failure partway is still reported truthfully.
- **The confirmation** says how many photos go to the Recycle Bin or Trash, that their edits, tags and ratings go with them and where to restore them, and BRW-9's sentence for photos in a synced folder, naming the service. Cancel has the focus.
- **Keys:** Delete on Windows. On the Mac it's Cmd+Delete, as in Finder and Photos, from a new File menu item, Move to Trash…. That shortcut would otherwise take Cmd+Delete away from the search box, so in a text field the command deletes to the start of the line instead, as the field would.
- **The Viewer** moves on to the next photo, or the one before at the end of the list.
- **Not undoable** (BRW-11). The Recycle Bin or Trash is the way back.
- **Still to measure:** the Mac from the app itself (the tool from the terminal was refused even for downloaded photos), and on Windows, a drive with no Recycle Bin, where Windows itself asks to delete permanently.
- **The design question is #103,** for a design session.
