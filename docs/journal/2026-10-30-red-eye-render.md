# Red-eye in the renderer (M4 slice 7a)

- **Slice 7 is split like slice 5**, Claude Code's plan: 7a draws red-eye corrections in the renderer, and 7b is red-eye mode, which waits for the design session's answers. The questions went to the design session (whether it's a mode like crop, what a click and a drag make, moving and removing circles, a limit on how many, the panel's entry). Space-drag panning waits for 7b too, since nothing uses the drag before red-eye mode.
- **The formula, Claude Code's, written into the appendix** (edit-format, `seura.redEye.md`): only red pixels change. How red a pixel is, its red's lead over the larger of green and blue as a share of its red, decides how far its red comes down to that larger one. The correction starts at 0.7 and is full at 0.9. In linear light, red pupils measure above 0.85 and even reddish skin stays below about 0.76, so a generous circle leaves skin, irises and catchlights alone. Each circle's edge is soft over its outer fifth, overlapping circles take the largest weight, and a circle of radius 0 corrects nothing.
- **A wrong first set of test colors.** The first red pupils in the vectors were pinkish, with blue a quarter of red, and were barely corrected. Real red pupils in linear light have green and blue far below red. The vectors now use those, plus a reddish skin (a little) and a brown iris (none).
- **The circles ride in the uniform block,** as a fixed array of 64 after the other fields, so WebGPU and WebGL2 share one layout. The generator that writes the preview's copy of the layout learned arrays. 64 is a limit the format doesn't have yet; it's one of the design questions. A file with more keeps its first 64.
- **The render check's first circles changed almost nothing:** 15 pixels. The check image's ramps are red only where red is near 1, and the check's +0.7 brightness clips those either way. Claude Code painted a small shaded red pupil into the check image, inside the check's crop. Its circle now changes over 100 pixels, and a test makes sure it keeps doing so. The render check passed on Windows (largest difference 1 level on WebGPU, 0 on WebGL2) and on Nevada's MacBook (0 on both, so WebKit's WebGL2 takes the 64-entry array), run by the Mac session, where every UI and Rust check also passed.
