# Thumbnail speed, the cache sweep, and scanning off the lock (M2 slice 6a)

- **Measured before changing anything.** Claude wrote a benchmark that makes 48 photo-like 12-megapixel JPEGs and runs the app's real thumbnail workers on them. On Nevada's PC (15 worker threads) it made 46 thumbnails a second: above PRF-3's 30, below the aim of 60.
- **It scaled badly:** 15 threads should have managed well over 100 a second. Resizing began by copying the whole decoded image, about 48 MB a photo, and that much memory traffic on every thread was the likely bottleneck. Resizing straight from the decoded image raised it to 67 a second, with no other change.
- **Claude then called scaled decoding unnecessary, and CI proved that wrong.** The plan's idea was to decode JPEGs at a half, quarter, or eighth of their size, which our decoder (zune-jpeg) can't do; the one that can would be a second JPEG decoder just for thumbnails. With the aim met, Claude left it out and put the benchmark in CI, with a pass mark of 30. Every CI runner failed it: 10 to 15 a second with two or three worker threads. That's a hint of what a four-core laptop would manage: near the limit or under it.
- **Nevada agreed to both of Claude's fixes:** scaled decoding with `jpeg-decoder` (thumbnails only; anything it can't read goes to the main decoder, which gives the real error), and CI reporting the number instead of failing on it, since its runners say little about the reference laptop. On Nevada's PC: 105 a second. One photo alone gained less than expected (49 ms against 61); with photo-like detail, reading the compressed data costs about as much as everything else. The big gain is many threads touching far less memory.
- **Noticed on the way:** a JPEG cut off partway decodes, in both decoders, with the missing part filled in, in zune-jpeg's case half transparent. That predates this slice and is left as it is for now.
- **Scanning off the lock** (carried from slice 3b): a scan used to hold the index for a whole library folder, so on a large first scan, clicking a folder waited for the walk to finish. Now folders are listed without the lock, and the lock is taken for one folder's photos at a time. Scans run one at a time, so a watcher's scan can't add a photo that a longer scan then removes as unseen. If a library folder is removed while it's being scanned, the scan stops instead of putting it back.
- **The cache sweep** Nevada added to slice 6: after the launch scan, thumbnails no photo in the library uses, and that haven't changed for a week, are deleted. It only touches files named the way Seura names thumbnails. Photos on a disconnected drive are still in the library, so their thumbnails stay.
