# Thumbnail speed, the cache sweep, and scanning off the lock (M2 slice 6a)

- **Measured before changing anything.** Claude wrote a benchmark that makes 48 photo-like 12-megapixel JPEGs and runs the app's real thumbnail workers on them. On Nevada's PC (15 worker threads) it made 46 thumbnails a second: above PRF-3's 30, below the aim of 60. One photo took 56 ms to decode, 23 ms to resize, and 12 ms to encode.
- **The surprise was how badly it scaled:** 15 threads should have managed well over 100 a second. Resizing began by copying the whole decoded image, about 48 MB a photo, and that much memory traffic on every thread was the likely bottleneck. Resizing straight from the decoded image raised it to 67 a second, with no other change.
- **Scaled decoding is on hold.** The plan's idea was to decode JPEGs at a half or an eighth of their size. Our decoder can't do that, and the one that can would mean a second JPEG decoder just for thumbnails. Claude decided it isn't worth that while the aim is met; the benchmark runs in CI, so a slower machine missing the target will show.
- **Scanning off the lock** (carried from slice 3b): a scan used to hold the index for a whole library folder, so on a large first scan, clicking a folder waited for the walk to finish. Now folders are listed without the lock, and the lock is taken for one folder's photos at a time. Scans run one at a time, so a watcher's scan can't add a photo that a longer scan then removes as unseen. If a library folder is removed while it's being scanned, the scan stops instead of putting it back.
- **The cache sweep** Nevada added to slice 6: after the launch scan, thumbnails no photo in the library uses, and that haven't changed for a week, are deleted. It only touches files named the way Seura names thumbnails. Photos on a disconnected drive are still in the library, so their thumbnails stay.
